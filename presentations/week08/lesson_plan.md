# Plan for Week 08

**Topic:** Ethical Concerns (AI, Plagiarism) and Review of KS Protocol
**Date:** April 17, 2026
**Format:** Lecture + discussion, instructor-led

## Opening: Data Check-In (~20 min)

- Revisit FAIR principles from Week 2 — students have now submitted Assignment #2 and should have data in hand
- Open discussion: Do you know what your data is? Have you collected it in a transparent way? Have you stored it appropriately? Are you prepared to analyze it?
- Self-assessment exercise: students evaluate their own data readiness against the FAIR framework
- This is a known challenge for students at this stage — the transition from planning to doing

## AI and Ethics in Research (~40 min)

- Context: why we're talking about this now (students are deep in independent research)
- Overview of Leiden University Faculty of Humanities GenAI guidelines (August 2025)
- Three-part structure:
  1. **What you CAN do:** spelling/grammar checks, machine translation of your own text, brainstorming with supervisor permission — always with disclosure
  2. **Best practices:** verify all AI output, be aware of hallucinations and bias, keep prompt/output logs, disclose everything, treat AI as a tool not an author
  3. **What you CANNOT do:** submit AI-generated text as your own, upload confidential/copyrighted data, paraphrase AI output without citation, assume permission carries across courses, use AI to rewrite someone else's text
- Plagiarism: quick review of university regulations — internet sources held to same standard as traditional sources
- Discussion: Have you used AI tools in your research? How? What went well or poorly?
- Scenario exercise: students evaluate real-world cases

## Thesis Protocol Review (~25 min)

- Where we are: Assignment #2 submitted, peer review done (Week 7), now working toward Assignment #3 (Empirical Draft, due May 06)
- What Assignment #3 requires: empirical chapter applying research design, initial data analysis, integration with prior sections
- Walk through the four assessment criteria from the protocol:
  1. Knowledge and insight (question, literature)
  2. Application of knowledge (data, methods, analysis)
  3. Reaching conclusions (synthesis, contribution)
  4. Communication (writing, structure, citations)
- Example thesis structure — where the empirical chapter fits
- Grade descriptors: what distinguishes a 7 from an 8 from a 9
- Looking ahead: Assignment #3 → final manuscript timeline

## Wrap-Up (~5 min)

- Key dates: Assignment #3 due May 06; no class Weeks 9-10 (free research time)
- Week 11 (May 08): in-class review and consultations — last class session
- Final manuscript due June 01
